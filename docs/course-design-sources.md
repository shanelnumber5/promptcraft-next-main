# Course-design sources

PromptCraft uses the sources below to explain its design choices. The lemonade story is a fictional illustration created for this module. These sources support the underlying principles; they do not evaluate PromptCraft, prove the effectiveness of every revision, or establish the value of AI use in a particular course.

| Teaching move | Source | Application in PromptCraft |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Align the activity and assessment with the learning goal. | Carnegie Mellon University, Eberly Center. [Why should assessments, learning objectives, and instructional strategies be aligned?](https://www.cmu.edu/teaching/assessment/basics/alignment.html) (n.d.). | Ask instructors for a learning goal and preserve requirements when revising directions. |
| Structure a scenario-based exchange around a decision students can examine. | Darabi, A., Arrastia, M. C., Nelson, D. W., Cornille, T., & Liang, X. (2011). [Cognitive presence in asynchronous online learning: A comparison of four discussion strategies](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2729.2010.00392.x). *Journal of Computer Assisted Learning, 27*(3), 216–227. | The study compares discussion strategies and finds different cognitive-presence patterns. The lemonade example applies the principle by assigning peers a decision to question; that exact example was not studied. |
| Make students' use of feedback visible. | Carless, D., & Boud, D. (2018). [The development of student feedback literacy: Enabling uptake of feedback](https://doi.org/10.1080/02602938.2018.1463354). *Assessment & Evaluation in Higher Education, 43*(8), 1315–1325. | Their conceptual framework includes judgment and action on feedback. PromptCraft applies it through a reasoned revision or defense after peer input. |

The interface and downloaded guide link to the sources near the relevant teaching moves. Babbage receives the same verified references and can cite them in its teacher-facing reason. They are not disciplinary sources for a geology, literature, or other course assignment. Babbage must use the instructor's own course evidence for those topics rather than invent references.

Publisher abstracts/full text and the institutional guidance were checked on October 12, 2026. The 2011 Darabi article was first published online in November 2010; references here use its journal issue year.
